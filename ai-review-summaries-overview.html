The Hidden Cost of AI-Generated Review Summaries

As major platforms (e.g., Amazon) invest billions in generative AI, AI-generated review summaries are becoming increasingly common in the digital customer journey. But could this convenience come at an unexpected cost? This case study finds out the answer using real-world platform data and eleven experiments.

The question

Online platforms increasingly use AI to summarise large volumes of customer reviews. These summaries are valuable to readers because they reduce information overload and make it easier to understand overall customer sentiment.

But platforms face a two-sided challenge: the same reviews that AI summarises must first be created by consumers.

The project therefore focused on three questions:

  1. Does introducing AI-generated review summaries reduce consumers' willingness to contribute reviews?
  2. Why would the presence of an AI summary change how much effort people put into writing?
  3. Can platform design preserve the benefits of AI summaries without discouraging user-generated content?

The study

Design

A multi-method research programme combining observational platform data with controlled behavioural experiments.

Sample

Field data
42,274 text reviews from 188 hotels across five U.S. leisure destinations, observed over 312 days, resulting in 117,312 observations.

Experiments
11 experiments completed with a total of 5,114 participants.

Analysis

  • Difference-in-differences
  • Fixed-effects modelling
  • Between-subjects experiments
  • ANOVA
  • Mediation analysis
  • Moderated mediation
  • Robustness checks

02

The issue was not AI itself — it was recognition

Why were consumers less motivated to write?

The experiments showed that people expected their individual reviews to receive less attention, appreciation and usefulness when an AI summary was already available.

03

Platform design can reduce the downside

When the interface highlighted ways readers could engage with individual reviews (such as helpful votes, comments and visible acknowledgement), the negative effect of AI summaries became substantially weaker.

Without engagement cues, AI summaries reduced willingness to leave a review from 4.99 to 4.39 (p < .001). When the interface highlighted ways readers could engage with individual reviews, the gap narrowed to 4.75 vs. 4.59 and was no longer significant (p = .37).

Recommendations

01

Measure both sides of the AI experience

Do not evaluate AI summaries only through reader-side metrics such as convenience, engagement or conversion.

Track contributor-side metrics too — particularly review volume, review length and contribution rates — to identify whether improvements in content consumption are weakening content creation.

02

Make individual contributions visible

Pair AI-generated summaries with clear signals that individual reviews still matter.

Surface features such as helpful votes, comments, replies and other visible acknowledgement close to individual reviews so contributors can see that readers continue to engage with their content.

03

Test the ecosystem, not just the feature

Before a full rollout, A/B test AI-summary designs against both reader and contributor outcomes.

The best design is not simply the one that makes reviews easiest to consume — it is the one that balances information efficiency with continued user participation.
