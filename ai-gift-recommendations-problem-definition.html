Problem Definition

AI Gift Recommendations & Consumer Adoption · Step 1 of 5

Understanding why consumers hesitate to use AI-powered gift recommendation tools when choosing gifts for people they care about.

Central Business Question

How can AI gift recommendation platforms encourage adoption when consumers' decisions are influenced not only by product suitability, but also by the emotional meaning of gift giving?

01 / CONTEXT

AI Gift Recommendations: Business Context and Research Purpose

Business Context

AI-powered recommendation systems are transforming how consumers discover products and make purchasing decisions.

In gift shopping, AI recommendation tools promise to simplify the selection process by generating personalised suggestions based on information about the recipient, such as age, interests, preferences, and the gift-giving occasion.

For businesses, these technologies offer opportunities to improve product discovery and support consumers who find gift selection difficult or time-consuming.

However, gift giving differs from ordinary shopping.

Consumers are not simply purchasing a product that satisfies their own needs. They are selecting something for another person, often with the intention of expressing care, appreciation, or emotional connection.

This creates a distinctive challenge for AI gift recommendation platforms: a recommendation may be functionally appropriate without fully satisfying the social and emotional expectations surrounding the gift.

Two Dimensions of Gift-Giving Value

Functional Value

Finding the Right Gift

Consumers want to identify gifts that match recipients' needs, interests, and personal preferences.

AI recommendation tools can potentially simplify the search process by identifying relevant options.

Relational Value

Expressing the Relationship

Consumers also want their gifts to communicate thoughtfulness, personal knowledge, and the importance of their relationship with the recipient.

Delegating gift selection to AI may raise concerns about whether the gift still expresses the intended relational meaning.

Why This Matters

Many AI gift recommendation platforms emphasise convenience, product suitability, and personalisation.

However, consumers may evaluate AI assistance differently depending on whom they are buying for.

A gift for an acquaintance may primarily require finding something suitable and affordable. A gift for a close friend may involve stronger expectations of personal effort, shared knowledge, and emotional significance.

As a result, the same AI recommendation tool may be perceived differently across social relationships.

For businesses, this raises an important question:

Does the effectiveness of an AI gift recommendation experience depend on the relationship between the gift giver and the recipient?

Research Purpose

This project investigates how social closeness between gift givers and recipients influences consumers' willingness to use AI gift recommendation tools.

Specifically, the research aims to:

  • Examine whether relationship closeness influences consumers' likelihood of using AI gift recommendation tools.
  • Investigate whether expected relational signalling and expected preference matching help explain this relationship.
  • Examine how consumer characteristics, gift-giving contexts, and AI interface capabilities shape these psychological processes.

02 / PROBLEM FRAMING

From Business Problem to Research Problem

The business challenge can be translated into two related but distinct questions: the decision facing AI gift recommendation companies and the consumer evidence needed to inform that decision.

Management Decision Problem

How should AI gift recommendation platforms design and position their services for consumers selecting gifts across different types of social relationships?

This decision involves determining whether a standardised recommendation experience can address different gift-giving needs or whether greater personalisation and relationship-sensitive interaction are required.

It also raises questions about which audiences and gift-giving occasions are most appropriate for different AI recommendation experiences.

Potential business decisions include:

  • Whether to incorporate relationship closeness into the gift recommendation process.
  • How to position AI assistance for close friends versus acquaintances.
  • Whether conversational AI features justify further product development and testing.
  • How to communicate the value of AI without undermining the personal meaning of gift selection.

Marketing Research Problem

Determine how social closeness between gift givers and recipients influences consumers' likelihood of using AI gift recommendation tools.

Investigate the roles of expected relational signalling and expected preference matching in explaining this relationship.

Examine whether the underlying psychological processes vary according to gift givers' self-oriented perfectionism, disclosure of the giver's identity, and the turn-taking capability of the AI recommendation tool.

04 / RESEARCH PRIORITIES

Four Questions Guide the Research

The study addresses four research priorities that connect consumer psychology with AI product adoption.

1. Social Closeness and AI Adoption

Examine whether consumers' willingness to use AI gift recommendation tools depends on how close they are to the intended gift recipient.

Gift shopping for a close friend may involve different expectations from gift shopping for a casual acquaintance.

The research compares consumers selecting gifts for close friends with those selecting gifts for distant, or "hi-bye", friends.

Key Question

Does greater social closeness between gift giver and recipient reduce willingness to use AI gift recommendation tools?

2. The Psychological Motivations Behind AI Adoption

Investigate two motivations influencing consumers' evaluations of AI gift recommendation tools.

Expected Relational Signalling

The extent to which consumers expect using an AI recommendation tool to help acknowledge or express their relationship with the recipient.

Expected Preference Matching

The extent to which consumers expect using AI to help identify a gift that matches the recipient's preferences.

These motivations capture different concerns: the personal meaning communicated through a gift and the expected suitability of the recommended product.

Key Question

Do expected relational signalling and expected preference matching help explain the relationship between social closeness and willingness to use AI?

3. Consumer Characteristics and Gift-Giving Context

Examine whether consumers' responses to AI recommendations depend on personal characteristics and expectations surrounding the gift-giving occasion.

Two factors are particularly relevant:

Self-Oriented Perfectionism

Some consumers hold especially demanding standards for their gift choices.

Their expectations of finding a suitable gift through AI may differ depending on the closeness of the recipient relationship.

Gift-Giver Identity Disclosure

In settings such as Secret Santa, gift givers may be identified or remain anonymous.

Whether the recipient knows who selected the gift may change the relational meaning associated with using AI assistance.

Key Question

How do perfectionism and gift-giver identity disclosure influence the psychological motivations underlying AI recommendation use?

4. AI Interface Design and Conversational Turn-Taking

Examine whether the way an AI recommendation tool interacts with consumers changes how they evaluate its ability to recommend suitable gifts.

Conventional recommendation interfaces may present several questions simultaneously.

Conversational interfaces can instead ask questions sequentially, allowing consumers to provide information through an interactive exchange.

This design feature may be particularly relevant when selecting gifts that require a nuanced understanding of the recipient.

Key Question

Can conversational turn-taking improve expected preference matching and consumers' willingness to use AI gift recommendation tools, particularly for close relationships?

05 / SCOPE

Scope and Boundaries

In Scope

  • Consumers' intentions to use AI gift recommendation tools.
  • Social closeness between gift givers and recipients.
  • Close friends versus distant or hi-bye friends.
  • Expected relational signalling.
  • Expected preference matching.
  • Self-oriented perfectionism as a consumer characteristic.
  • Disclosure versus non-disclosure of the gift giver's identity.
  • Conversational turn-taking in AI recommendation interfaces.
  • Birthday, wedding, Secret Santa, and housewarming scenarios.
  • Evidence from five experimental studies.

Out of Scope

  • Actual purchases made through commercial AI gift recommendation platforms.
  • Real-world conversion rates, revenue, or customer retention.
  • Longitudinal AI recommendation adoption.
  • Technical assessment of recommendation algorithm accuracy.
  • Direct comparison of every commercially available gift recommendation product.
  • Consumer satisfaction with gifts after they are received.
  • Recipient evaluations of AI-selected gifts.
  • Financial ROI from implementing conversational AI features.

Interpretation Boundary

The research primarily investigates gift givers' self-reported likelihood of using AI recommendation tools, rather than tracking actual usage behaviour on a commercial platform.

The psychological motivations are measured using participants' expectations of AI-assisted gift selection.

Accordingly, the research addresses how consumers anticipate the value and suitability of AI recommendations. It does not establish the objective quality of AI-generated gift suggestions or the satisfaction of recipients.

The experimental findings can inform decisions about product design, positioning, and consumer targeting, but additional real-world testing would be required to evaluate implementation outcomes.

The studies also distinguish changes in psychological pathways from changes in the overall effect on willingness to use AI. A moderator affecting expected preference matching or relational signalling does not necessarily establish a significant change in the total effect on usage intention.

06 / WHAT COMES NEXT

How Problem Definition Connects to the Research Process

01 · Problem Definition02 · Research Approach03 · Research Design04 · Analysis05 · Report

The problem definition establishes the business challenge, research objectives, and evidence needed to understand consumers' adoption of AI gift recommendation tools.

The following stages translate these questions into a structured empirical investigation.

01 — Problem Definition

Define the challenge of encouraging AI recommendation adoption in a context where purchasing decisions carry interpersonal meaning.

02 — Research Approach

Develop a dual-motives framework connecting social closeness with expected relational signalling, expected preference matching, and willingness to use AI gift recommendation tools.

Identify the proposed roles of self-oriented perfectionism, identity disclosure, and conversational turn-taking.

03 — Research Design

Design five experiments across different gift-giving occasions.

Manipulate social closeness, identity disclosure, and AI turn-taking where relevant, and measure the psychological motivations underlying AI recommendation use.

04 — Analysis

Examine differences in usage intentions, investigate the proposed mediation pathways, and test moderation and moderated mediation effects.

Distinguish findings about overall usage intentions from findings about underlying psychological mechanisms.

05 — Report: Insights and Recommendations

Translate the evidence into recommendations for relationship-based personalisation, AI gift recommendation interface design, and marketing communication.
