AI Gift Recommendations & Consumer Adoption · Step 5 of 5

From consumer psychology to product strategy: recommendations for designing AI-powered gift recommendation experiences that reflect the social meaning of gift giving.

01 / EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Challenge: AI Recommendations Are Not Just About Finding the Right Product

AI-powered gift recommendation tools promise to simplify gift shopping by helping consumers find products that match recipients' preferences.

However, gift giving is more than a product selection task. It is also a social act through which consumers express care, personal knowledge, and the meaning of their relationships.

This creates a challenge for AI recommendation platforms: a tool that makes gift shopping more efficient may not be equally appealing across different social relationships.

What the Research Found

Across five experimental studies involving 1,293 participants, the research found that social closeness plays an important role in consumers' willingness to use AI gift recommendation tools.

Consumers were less willing to use AI when choosing gifts for close friends than for distant friends in the initial experiment.

Two psychological motivations help explain these responses:

Expected Relational Signaling

Whether consumers believe AI-assisted gift selection can communicate the meaning of their relationship with the recipient.

Expected Preference Matching

Whether consumers believe AI can identify a gift that reflects the recipient's personal preferences.

Further experiments identified conditions under which these motivations vary, including self-oriented perfectionism, gift-giver identity disclosure, and AI conversational turn-taking.

Strategic Recommendation

Design AI gift recommendations around relationships, not just recipient characteristics.

AI platforms should consider how close the giver is to the recipient, what the giver wants the gift to communicate, and how the recommendation experience can accommodate these different motivations.

The research suggests opportunities for relationship-aware personalisation, conversational interfaces, and gift-occasion-specific positioning.

These opportunities require additional product testing to establish their effects on actual adoption and commercial performance.

The Central Insight

The value of an AI gift recommendation depends partly on the relationship behind the gift.

When choosing gifts for distant friends, consumers may particularly value assistance in finding appropriate products despite limited knowledge of the recipient.

When choosing gifts for close friends, consumers may place greater emphasis on expressing personal care and ensuring that recommendations reflect the recipient's unique preferences.

A recommendation experience that overlooks these relational differences may fail to address important consumer expectations.

03 / STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATIONS

Recommendation 01 — Introduce Relationship-Aware Personalisation

Evidence: Studies 1 and 2

Make the giver–recipient relationship part of the recommendation experience

Many gift recommendation tools ask about the recipient's age, interests, hobbies, and the gift-giving occasion.

The research suggests that another variable may matter: the social closeness between the giver and the recipient.

Recommended actions

Allow consumers to optionally indicate their relationship with the recipient during the recommendation process.

For example, the tool could ask whether the gift is intended for a close friend, casual acquaintance, or colleague.

Use this information to explore different recommendation experiences.

For close relationships

Offer opportunities to provide more detailed personal information about the recipient's interests, preferences, and shared experiences.

For distant relationships

Prioritise straightforward information gathering and suggestions that meet the occasion's requirements without demanding extensive personal knowledge.

Business rationale

Relationship-aware recommendation experiences may better reflect the different motivations consumers bring to gift shopping.

Evidence boundary

The experiments demonstrate the relevance of relationship closeness to consumer perceptions and intentions. They do not directly test a production recommendation system that uses relationship information to personalise results.

Recommendation 02 — Address Both Relational Signaling and Preference Matching

Evidence: Study 2

Design for the meaning of the gift as well as its suitability

The dual motives framework identifies two reasons consumers may hesitate to use AI recommendations.

A consumer may believe that AI is capable of identifying suitable products but still question whether relying on AI is compatible with expressing personal care.

Conversely, a consumer may welcome assistance with gift selection while doubting whether AI understands the recipient well enough.

These concerns should be evaluated separately.

Recommended actions

Develop recommendation experiences that allow consumers to supplement structured recipient information with personally meaningful details.

Potential features to test include:

  • Adding shared memories or meaningful experiences.
  • Describing the recipient's unique interests and preferences.
  • Explaining why a particular gift would be meaningful.
  • Allowing consumers to refine recommendations based on personal knowledge.
  • Supporting personalisation without replacing the giver's own judgement.

Business rationale

A relationship-sensitive interface may be better suited to gift-giving decisions that involve both functional and emotional goals.

Evidence boundary

The psychological motivations were examined experimentally, but these proposed features were not independently tested in the published studies.

Recommendation 03 — Consider Gift-Giving Occasions and Identity Disclosure

Evidence: Study 4

Recognise the different social expectations of anonymous and identified gift exchanges

Study 4 examined a Secret Santa context in which the gift giver's identity was either disclosed or undisclosed.

For close colleagues, non-disclosure was associated with higher expected relational signaling and preference matching when considering AI assistance.

This suggests that the social visibility of the gift giver can influence expectations about using an AI recommendation tool.

Recommended actions

Investigate gift recommendation experiences designed for occasions involving different levels of anonymity.

For example, AI platforms could explore dedicated experiences for Secret Santa, workplace gift exchanges, and group celebrations.

Where relevant, product messaging could emphasise:

  • Finding suitable gifts for colleagues.
  • Simplifying unfamiliar recipient preferences.
  • Making gift selection easier for anonymous exchanges.
  • Choosing appropriate gifts within a specified occasion and budget.

Business rationale

Gift-giving occasions differ in the social meaning attached to the giver's identity and the expectations surrounding the selected gift.

Evidence boundary

Study 4 supported moderated indirect pathways through the psychological motivations. It did not establish a significant social-closeness × identity-disclosure interaction on the direct path to AI usage intention.

The study also did not test real commercial Secret Santa campaigns.

Recommendation 04 — Test Conversational Turn-Taking for Close Relationships

Evidence: Study 5

Move beyond static questionnaires when greater personalisation is needed

Study 5 examined whether the presentation of recipient-related questions influenced expectations of AI recommendation suitability.

Participants encountered one of two versions of an AI recommendation interface.

Without Turn-Taking

All eight questions appeared simultaneously.

With Turn-Taking

The same questions were presented sequentially, creating a conversational exchange.

For close friends, expected preference matching was significantly higher with turn-taking.

Without turn-taking3.85
With turn-taking4.58
Difference+0.73

Study 5, close-friend condition; seven-point scale, p = .005.

The corresponding difference was not significant for hi-bye friends.

Recommended actions

Prototype a conversational gift recommendation experience that progressively collects information about the recipient.

Evaluate whether sequential dialogue improves the consumer's sense that the AI understands the recipient's preferences.

Compare the conversational interface with a conventional questionnaire using the same information requirements.

Business rationale

Turn-taking offers a concrete interface design feature that can be evaluated experimentally.

It may be especially relevant when gift givers want to provide detailed information about someone they know well.

Evidence boundary

Study 5 established an improvement in expected preference matching, alongside a significant moderated indirect pathway.

It did not demonstrate a significant interaction on the direct path predicting AI usage intention, nor did it measure an increase in actual adoption or conversion.

Proposed product concepts

Quick Gift Finder

For more distant relationships, ask for a few recipient details and suggest broadly suitable gifts.

Personal Gift Assistant

For closer relationships, use conversational questions to learn about personal preferences and the relationship.

How Should AI Recommendation Platforms Evaluate These Opportunities?

The following measurement framework is proposed for future commercial testing.

It extends the psychological measures used in the research with behavioural and product-performance indicators.

Primary Research Metrics

The original experiments provide established measures for three outcomes that can be used in an initial product test:

Likelihood to Use AI

A ten-point measure of consumers' intention to use the recommendation tool.

Expected Relational Signaling

A seven-point measure of whether consumers believe using AI can help express their relationship with the recipient.

Expected Preference Matching

A seven-point measure of whether consumers believe the AI can identify a gift matching the recipient's preferences.

Behavioural Validation

For a real-world implementation, self-reported measures should be supplemented with behavioural evidence.

Relevant outcomes may include whether consumers start a recommendation session, complete the interaction, select a recommended gift, or proceed to purchase.

These measures would help establish whether improvements in psychological expectations translate into meaningful product outcomes.

Suggested Evaluation Principles

Before running a commercial experiment, define the intended audience, comparison conditions, primary outcome, and sample size.

Measure the possible benefits of richer personalisation alongside its costs, such as additional time, effort, and information requirements.

Evaluate outcomes separately for close versus distant relationships rather than relying only on an overall average.

The research does not establish a commercial conversion benchmark or a universal target for improving AI usage.

05 / PROPOSED ACTION PLAN

From Consumer Insight to Product Experimentation

A four-stage roadmap can translate the research findings into a practical product development process.

01 · Audit02 · Prototype03 · Experiment04 · Decide

Step 01 — Audit the Existing Recommendation Experience

Objective

Identify whether the current recommendation process accounts for the social relationship between gift giver and recipient.

Proposed activities

Review onboarding questions, recipient information fields, recommendation flows, and marketing messages.

Map where the experience currently considers relationship context, recipient preferences, and opportunities for personalisation.

Deliverable

A research-informed assessment of the current recommendation journey and potential gaps in relationship-sensitive design.

Step 02 — Develop Two Experience Concepts

Objective

Explore whether different recommendation experiences are appropriate for different gift-giving relationships.

Concept A — Quick Gift Finder

A streamlined interface designed for consumers seeking suitable gifts with limited recipient information.

Potential use cases include acquaintances, workplace colleagues, and routine gift exchanges.

Concept B — Personal Gift Assistant

A conversational interface that gathers more detailed information about the recipient and allows consumers to incorporate personal knowledge.

Potential use cases include close friends and emotionally meaningful occasions.

Deliverable

Two testable recommendation prototypes with clearly defined information requirements and interaction flows.

These concepts are proposed applications of the findings, not interfaces validated in the original research.

Step 03 — Run a Controlled Product Experiment

Objective

Determine whether the proposed experiences improve consumers' expectations and actual use of AI recommendations.

Suggested design

Randomly assign participants to the experience variants.

Measure outcomes separately for consumers choosing gifts for close and distant recipients.

Where possible, keep recommendation content, information requirements, and underlying technology consistent so that differences can be attributed to the tested interface features.

Primary outcomes

Expected preference matching, expected relational signaling, and AI usage intention.

Secondary outcomes

Session completion, recommendation selection, perceived effort, and relevant purchasing behaviour.

Deliverable

Experimental evidence comparing the two experiences, including any differences across relationship types.

Step 04 — Refine and Evaluate Business Value

Objective

Use experimental results to decide which features merit further investment.

Proposed activities

Identify which experience provides the greatest benefit for each target audience.

Assess whether additional conversational features improve consumer perceptions without creating unnecessary friction.

Compare behavioural performance and user feedback alongside any additional development and operating costs.

Deliverable

An evidence-based recommendation on whether to implement, revise, or further test the proposed features.

Decision Principle

Invest in relationship-sensitive features when controlled evidence shows that they improve relevant consumer or business outcomes sufficiently to justify their implementation costs.

Do not assume that stronger personalisation is always better. Its value may depend on the recipient relationship and the type of gift-giving decision.

06 / LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE RESEARCH

What the Research Can and Cannot Establish

The five experiments offer evidence that social closeness, consumer motivations, gift-giving contexts, and AI interaction design are relevant to consumers' intentions to use AI gift recommendation tools.

However, several limitations are important when applying the findings to commercial platforms.

01 — Intention Versus Actual Behaviour

The research assessed consumers' reported likelihood of using AI gift recommendation tools.

It did not observe actual product adoption, completed purchases, or post-purchase outcomes.

Future research should test whether the identified psychological mechanisms predict real decisions.

02 — Hypothetical Gift-Giving Scenarios

The experiments used controlled scenarios involving birthdays, weddings, Secret Santa, and housewarming.

Participants did not interact with a fully functioning AI gift recommendation platform to select and purchase actual gifts.

Real-world product testing would help evaluate whether the effects generalise beyond experimental scenarios.

03 — Cultural Generalisability

The research recruited participants in the United Kingdom through Prolific.

Gift-giving norms and expectations about AI may vary across countries and cultures.

Cross-cultural research could investigate whether social closeness has similar effects in other markets.

04 — AI Versus Human Recommendations

The research examined willingness to use AI gift recommendation tools but did not directly compare them with recommendations from human advisers.

Future studies could investigate whether the same relationship-sensitive responses emerge when consumers choose between AI and human assistance.

05 — The Recipient Perspective

The five studies focused on gift givers.

They did not examine how recipients evaluate gifts selected with AI assistance.

Future research could investigate whether AI involvement changes perceived thoughtfulness, appreciation, or satisfaction from the recipient's perspective.

06 — Further Interface Design Research

Study 5 examined conversational turn-taking by comparing sequential and simultaneous presentation of the same questions.

Future studies could explore additional features, including adaptive follow-up questions, personal memory inputs, and different levels of AI conversational capability.

These features should be evaluated individually to determine which aspects of the interaction influence consumer perceptions and behaviour.

07 / FINAL CONCLUSION

Designing AI Recommendations for Socially Meaningful Decisions

AI-powered recommendation tools are often evaluated according to their efficiency, relevance, and ability to simplify purchasing decisions.

However, gift giving is not simply a search for the most suitable product.

It is a social activity in which consumers express care, personal knowledge, and the significance of their relationships.

Across five experiments, this research demonstrates that social closeness influences consumers' willingness to use AI gift recommendation tools and identifies two psychological motivations underlying their responses: expected relational signaling and expected preference matching.

The research also shows that these motivations vary with individual characteristics, gift-giving contexts, and conversational interface design.

These findings suggest that effective AI recommendation systems may need to account for both the functional and relational dimensions of consumer decisions.

Closing Recommendation

Make AI recommendations more personal by understanding the relationships behind the purchase.

For AI gift recommendation platforms, this means moving beyond demographic targeting and occasion-based suggestions to investigate more relationship-sensitive experiences.

A promising strategy combines three considerations:

Know the Relationship

Recognise that consumers' expectations of AI differ when choosing gifts for close friends and distant acquaintances.

Understand the Motivation

Consider both the desire to find a suitable gift and the need to communicate the meaning of a relationship.

Design the Interaction

Explore conversational and personalised recommendation experiences that respond to different gift-giving needs.

By applying these insights through controlled product experimentation, AI recommendation companies can make more informed decisions about customer segmentation, personalisation, and interface design.

Research Contribution

This project extends AI recommendation research beyond individual consumption by demonstrating how social relationships shape consumers' acceptance of AI assistance.

It also provides a framework for investigating how psychological motivations and technology design jointly influence consumer responses to AI in socially meaningful purchasing contexts.
