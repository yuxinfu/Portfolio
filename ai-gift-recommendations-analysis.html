Analysis

AI Gift Recommendations & Consumer Adoption · Step 4 of 5

Understanding how social closeness influences consumers' willingness to use AI gift recommendation tools, why these differences emerge, and when they depend on consumer characteristics, gift-giving contexts, and AI interface design.

The Analytical Story

Gift givers may hesitate to use AI recommendations for close friends because gift selection involves more than identifying an appropriate product. It also involves communicating the meaning of the relationship and demonstrating personal understanding of the recipient.

Across five experiments, the research identifies social closeness as an important consideration in AI recommendation adoption and examines the psychological pathways and boundary conditions behind this relationship.

01 / ANALYTICAL FRAMEWORK

From Experimental Data to Consumer Insights

The analysis addresses three connected research objectives.

1. Establish the Relationship

Determine whether social closeness influences consumers' willingness to use AI gift recommendation tools.

2. Explain the Underlying Motivations

Examine whether expected relational signaling and expected preference matching help explain how social closeness relates to AI adoption.

3. Investigate Boundary Conditions

Evaluate whether self-oriented perfectionism, gift-giver identity disclosure, and conversational turn-taking alter the psychological pathways associated with AI usage intention.

Interpreting the Evidence

Three types of statistical results are distinguished throughout this analysis:

Condition Comparisons

Differences in reported mean scores between experimental conditions.

Direct Effects

Estimated relationships between predictors and outcomes after accounting for other variables included in the statistical model.

Indirect Effects

Estimated pathways linking social closeness to AI usage intention through the proposed psychological mediators.

A statistically significant indirect effect does not necessarily imply a statistically significant overall effect or interaction on AI usage intention.

02 / STUDY 1: SOCIAL CLOSENESS AND AI ADOPTION

Does Relationship Closeness Affect Willingness to Use AI?

Study 1 · Birthday gift scenario · N = 209

The first experiment examined whether consumers were less willing to use AI gift recommendation tools when choosing gifts for close friends compared with hi-bye friends.

Participants considered using an AI gift recommendation tool to select a birthday gift for a recipient with whom they had either a close or distant relationship.

Results

Gift RecipientMean AI Usage IntentionSD
Close friend5.912.49
Hi-bye friend6.572.41
Difference−0.66

Mean scores on a ten-point scale; difference = close minus hi-bye.

A linear regression controlling for age and gender found a statistically significant effect of social closeness.

β = −0.68, SE = 0.34, t(205) = −2.02, p < .05.

Interpretation

Participants selecting gifts for close friends were less willing to use the AI gift recommendation tool than participants selecting gifts for hi-bye friends.

The result supports H1 and provides initial evidence that social closeness influences consumers' acceptance of AI assistance in gift selection.

Consumer Insight

The relationship between the purchaser and recipient can shape the perceived suitability of AI assistance.

Gift recommendation platforms should not assume that consumers evaluate AI recommendations in the same way across all recipient relationships.

03 / STUDY 2: WHY DOES SOCIAL CLOSENESS MATTER?

Two Psychological Motivations Behind AI Adoption

Study 2 · Birthday gift scenario · N = 269

Study 2 extended the initial experiment by measuring two proposed mediators:

  • Expected relational signaling.
  • Expected preference matching.

The analysis examined whether these motivations could explain the relationship between social closeness and willingness to use AI gift recommendation tools.

Step 1 — Compare Psychological Motivations

Participants in the close-friend condition reported lower expectations of the AI tool's ability to support both motivations.

Expected relational signaling3.664.69−1.03
Expected preference matching4.855.17−0.32

Mean scores on seven-point scales; differences = close minus hi-bye.

Social closeness significantly predicted expected relational signaling (β = −1.04, p < .001) and expected preference matching (β = −0.34, p = .03).

Step 2 — Test the Dual Mediation Model

A parallel mediation model was estimated using PROCESS Model 4 with 5,000 bootstrap samples.

Pathway A — Expected Relational Signaling

Gift givers selecting gifts for close friends reported lower expectations that AI-assisted selection would help express the relationship.

The indirect effect through expected relational signaling was statistically significant.

Pathway B — Expected Preference Matching

Gift givers selecting gifts for close friends also reported lower expectations that AI could identify gifts matching recipients' preferences.

The indirect effect through expected preference matching was statistically significant.

Step 4 — Interpret Usage Intention Separately

The reported descriptive means for AI usage intention were:

Gift RecipientMean
Close friend6.26
Hi-bye friend6.50

Although the mean usage intention was lower for close friends, this descriptive difference should not be treated as an independently established significant main effect.

The published mediation model also reported a significant adjusted direct effect (β = +0.51, p = .04). This coefficient represents the conditional relationship after accounting for the mediators and covariates; it is not the same as the unadjusted difference between the two experimental groups.

The strongest evidence from Study 2 is therefore the two significant indirect pathways and the significant differences in the proposed psychological motivations.

Interpretation

Consumers' responses to AI gift recommendations involve two distinct evaluations.

Relational Meaning

Can the AI-assisted selection process communicate the closeness and personal meaning of the relationship?

Recommendation Suitability

Can the AI identify a gift that accurately reflects the recipient's preferences?

When choosing gifts for close friends, participants reported lower expectations on both dimensions.

Consumer Insight

AI recommendation acceptance involves both functional and relational value.

Improving recommendation relevance alone may not fully address consumers' concerns when choosing gifts for emotionally important relationships.

04 / BOUNDARY CONDITIONS: WHEN DO THE MOTIVATIONS CHANGE?

Three additional experiments investigated whether consumer characteristics, gift-giving circumstances, and AI interface design alter the psychological processes underlying AI recommendation adoption.

Study 3 — Self-Oriented Perfectionism

N = 280 · Wedding gift scenario

Research Question

Does self-oriented perfectionism influence how social closeness affects expected preference matching?

Self-oriented perfectionism reflects the high standards consumers set for themselves when choosing gifts.

The analysis investigated whether these standards make consumers more sensitive to the recipient relationship when evaluating AI recommendations.

Key Result

The interaction between social closeness and self-oriented perfectionism significantly predicted expected preference matching.

β = −0.40, SE = 0.13, p = .002.

The corresponding moderated mediation index through preference matching was also significant.

Index = −0.49, 95% CI [−0.86, −0.15].

Expected Preference Matching by Perfectionism

PerfectionismClose FriendHi-Bye Friend
Low4.924.86
High4.405.23

Mean scores on seven-point scales; low and high represent values one standard deviation below and above the mean.

Among participants with high self-oriented perfectionism, expected preference matching was significantly lower for close friends than hi-bye friends (p = .001).

Among those with low perfectionism, the difference was not significant (p = .86).

The conditional indirect effect through preference matching was significant at high perfectionism (−1.04, 95% CI [−1.63, −0.51]) but not at low perfectionism (0.04, 95% CI [−0.42, 0.51]).

What Was Not Supported?

Self-oriented perfectionism did not significantly moderate the social-closeness effect on expected relational signaling (p = .36).

The interaction predicting AI usage intention was also not significant (p = .29).

Therefore, H3a received support for the preference-matching pathway, while H3b was not supported.

Consumer Insight

Consumers with demanding gift-selection standards may be especially sceptical of AI's ability to identify suitable gifts for close friends.

This suggests that AI recommendation platforms could investigate more detailed preference collection and personalisation for consumers who place high importance on selecting the ideal gift.

Study 4 — Gift-Giver Identity Disclosure

N = 256 · Workplace Secret Santa scenario

Research Question

Does the disclosure of a gift giver's identity influence the psychological motivations underlying AI recommendation adoption?

Study 4 examined a 2 × 2 experiment manipulating social closeness and whether the giver's identity was disclosed.

Key Result 1 — Relational Signaling

The interaction between social closeness and identity disclosure significantly predicted expected relational signaling.

β = −0.72, SE = 0.34, p = .03.

The corresponding moderated mediation index through relational signaling was significant.

Index = −0.22, 95% CI [−0.54, −0.01].

Expected Relational Signaling by Identity Disclosure

RelationshipIdentity UndisclosedIdentity Disclosed
Close colleague4.233.49
Hi-bye colleague4.014.00

For close colleagues, disclosing the giver's identity reduced expected relational signaling associated with using AI (p = .007).

For hi-bye colleagues, the difference was not significant (p = .95).

Key Result 2 — Preference Matching

Identity disclosure also significantly moderated the relationship between social closeness and expected preference matching.

β = −0.73, SE = 0.27, p = .009.

The moderated mediation index through preference matching was significant.

Index = −0.95, 95% CI [−1.65, −0.24].

For close colleagues, participants reported higher expected preference matching when their identity was undisclosed (5.54) than when it was disclosed (4.71).

The corresponding reported conditional regression effect was significant (p = .02).

What Was Not Supported?

Identity disclosure did not significantly moderate the relationship between social closeness and AI usage intention in the direct path of the model.

Interaction β = 0.15, p = .72.

Thus, H4a was supported for the relational-signaling pathway, while H4b was not supported.

Consumer Insight

The social visibility of gift giving can influence how consumers evaluate AI assistance.

In gift exchanges where the giver remains anonymous, consumers selecting gifts for close colleagues may have more favourable expectations of AI's ability to support relational signaling and preference matching.

This makes anonymous gift exchanges a potentially relevant context for AI recommendation platforms to investigate.

Study 5 — AI Conversational Turn-Taking

N = 279 · Housewarming gift scenario

Research Question

Can conversational turn-taking improve consumers' expectations of AI recommendation quality, particularly when selecting gifts for close friends?

Study 5 compared two AI interfaces:

Without Turn-Taking

All eight recipient-related questions were presented simultaneously.

With Turn-Taking

The same eight questions were presented sequentially, creating a more conversational interaction.

The information requested was held constant while the interaction format changed.

Key Result

Conversational turn-taking significantly moderated the relationship between social closeness and expected preference matching.

β = 0.76, SE = 0.33, p = .02.

The moderated mediation index through expected preference matching was also significant.

Index = 0.55, 95% CI [0.08, 1.12].

Expected Preference Matching by Interface

RelationshipWithout Turn-TakingWith Turn-TakingDifference
Close friend3.854.58+0.73
Hi-bye friend4.644.63−0.01

Mean scores on seven-point scales; difference = turn-taking present minus absent.

For close friends, conversational turn-taking significantly increased expected preference matching (p = .005).

For hi-bye friends, the difference was not significant (p = .99).

The conditional indirect effect through preference matching was significant without turn-taking (−0.57, 95% CI [−1.02, −0.21]) but not with turn-taking (−0.03, 95% CI [−0.37, 0.31]).

What Was Not Supported?

Turn-taking did not significantly moderate the social-closeness relationship with expected relational signaling (p = .83).

The social-closeness × turn-taking interaction predicting AI usage intention was not significant (p = .91).

Therefore, H5a was supported for the preference-matching pathway, while H5b was not supported.

Consumer Insight

The design of an AI recommendation interface can influence how capable the tool appears to consumers.

Sequential conversational interaction improved expected preference matching for close friends, suggesting that interface design may be especially important when consumers are selecting gifts requiring detailed personal knowledge.

However, Study 5 did not establish a significant moderation effect on the direct path predicting AI usage intention.

05 / CROSS-STUDY SYNTHESIS

What Do the Five Experiments Reveal?

The research programme provides evidence that social closeness affects consumers' responses to AI gift recommendation tools and that the underlying psychological motivations vary by consumer, context, and technology design.

Evidence Summary

Study 5Turn-taking improves expected preference matching for close friendsConditional effect p = .005; interaction p = .02

Three Core Insights

Insight 1 — Social Relationships Matter

The willingness to use AI recommendations is shaped by the relationship between the giver and the recipient.

AI tools may be evaluated more favourably for less emotionally close relationships.

Insight 2 — Consumers Evaluate Two Different Kinds of Value

Expected relational signaling and expected preference matching are distinct psychological motivations.

Consumers may question AI's ability to communicate relationship meaning even when they recognise its potential to recommend suitable products.

Insight 3 — Interface Design and Context Matter

Perfectionism, anonymity, and conversational turn-taking influence the psychological motivations underlying AI recommendation use.

These effects are not identical, and they should not be interpreted as universal increases in overall adoption intention.

06 / EVIDENCE QUALITY AND INTERPRETATION

Strengths of the Research

Multiple Experimental Studies

Five studies examined the research questions using independent participant samples and different gift-giving contexts.

Theory-Driven Mechanisms

The research differentiated relational signaling from preference matching rather than treating AI adoption as a single undifferentiated judgement.

Multiple Boundary Conditions

The studies examined an individual characteristic, a social-context characteristic, and a technology-design characteristic.

Controlled Interface Manipulation

Study 5 compared sequential and simultaneous question presentation while keeping the information requested constant.

Bootstrapped Indirect Effects

The mediation and moderated mediation models used 5,000 bootstrap samples to evaluate confidence intervals for indirect effects.

Interpretation Boundaries

Adoption Intentions, Not Actual Purchases

The primary outcome was self-reported likelihood of using an AI gift recommendation tool. Actual product use, gift purchases, and conversion rates were not measured.

Expected Rather Than Actual Recommendation Quality

Expected preference matching measured consumers' beliefs about the tool's ability to recommend suitable gifts. It did not measure objective recommendation accuracy.

Measured Psychological Mediators

Relational signaling and preference matching were measured rather than independently manipulated. The indirect effects are consistent with the proposed psychological explanations but do not establish definitive causal mediation.

Direct and Indirect Effects Are Different

The significant moderated mediation findings in Studies 3–5 do not establish significant moderation of the overall social-closeness effect on AI usage intention.

Sample and Context Boundaries

The experiments were conducted with United Kingdom Prolific participants using hypothetical gift-giving scenarios.

Further evidence would be required to establish how the findings translate to real purchasing environments and other consumer populations.

Analytical Takeaway

The evidence supports a relationship-sensitive approach to AI recommendation research.

However, the findings do not establish that adding conversational turn-taking, anonymising gift exchanges, or increasing personalisation will necessarily improve actual conversion or adoption rates.

Those commercial outcomes require further behavioural experimentation.

07 / FROM EVIDENCE TO BUSINESS IMPLICATIONS

Translating Consumer Psychology into Product Opportunities

The results identify several opportunities for AI recommendation platforms to investigate.

Opportunity 1 — Relationship-Aware Recommendation Experiences

Evidence: Social closeness affects consumers' willingness to use AI and their expectations of relational signaling and preference matching.

Potential application: Allow consumers to indicate their relationship with the recipient and explore whether different recommendation experiences work better for close versus distant relationships.

Opportunity 2 — More Personalised Information Collection

Evidence: Expected preference matching is an important psychological motivation, particularly when consumers select gifts for close friends.

Potential application: Explore questions about personal interests, shared experiences, and nuanced preferences that might improve consumers' confidence in gift recommendations.

Opportunity 3 — Context-Sensitive Positioning

Evidence: Identity disclosure influences relational signaling and preference-matching expectations in the Secret Santa context.

Potential application: Test AI recommendation experiences designed for anonymous or workplace gift exchanges, while retaining appropriate privacy protections.

Opportunity 4 — Conversational Interface Design

Evidence: Sequential turn-taking increased expected preference matching for close friends.

Potential application: Test conversational recommendation interfaces against conventional questionnaires and evaluate whether changes in perceived suitability translate into actual product use and purchasing behaviour.

Final Analytical Conclusion

AI recommendation adoption is not only about recommending the right product. It is also about understanding why the consumer is choosing it for someone else.

The research shows that the social relationship surrounding a purchase can influence consumers' expectations of AI assistance.

By distinguishing relational signaling from preference matching and examining how these motivations vary with personal characteristics, gift-giving circumstances, and interface design, the five experiments provide a foundation for developing and testing more relationship-sensitive AI recommendation experiences.
