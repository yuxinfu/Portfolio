Why Shoppers Hesitate to Use AI Gift Recommendations and What Retailers Can Do About It

Five experiments reveal how personal relationships shape AI adoption—and how retailers can rethink personalisation, conversational design, and gift recommendation experiences.

The Question

AI-powered recommendation systems are increasingly helping consumers discover products, compare options, and make purchasing decisions.

In gift shopping, these tools can suggest products based on a recipient's age, interests, preferences, or the occasion.

However, gift giving differs from ordinary purchasing decisions. A gift is not only valued for how well it matches the recipient's preferences, but also for what it communicates about the relationship.

For close friends, the act of choosing a gift may signal personal knowledge, care, and emotional investment. Using AI to assist with that decision could therefore create concerns that do not arise when choosing gifts for distant friends or acquaintances.

The project focused on three questions:

  • Does social closeness between the gift giver and recipient influence willingness to use AI gift recommendation tools?
  • Why does relationship closeness affect AI adoption, and what roles do relational signalling and preference matching play?
  • Can characteristics of gift givers, gift-giving contexts, and AI tool design help explain when consumers become more receptive to AI recommendations?

The Study

Design

A five-study experimental research programme examining how social relationships, consumer motivations, and AI design features influence gift givers' intentions to use AI recommendation tools.

Participants imagined selecting gifts for close friends or distant (hi-bye) friends across different gift-giving occasions.

The studies investigated both the psychological motivations underlying AI adoption and the conditions under which these motivations change.

Sample

Five experiments with 1,293 UK participants, recruited through Prolific and retained after study-specific exclusions.

What the Research Found

01 — Consumers were less willing to use AI when choosing gifts for close friends

The closer the relationship, the greater the potential resistance to AI-assisted gift selection.

Study 1 examined consumers' willingness to use an AI gift recommendation tool when choosing a birthday gift for a close friend versus a distant friend.

Gift RecipientMean
Close friend5.91
Hi-bye friend6.57

Mean scores on a 10-point scale; Study 1, N = 209. The difference was statistically significant (p < .05).

Participants were less inclined to rely on AI when selecting gifts for close friends.

Key insight: Consumers' willingness to adopt AI recommendations depends not only on the technology's functionality but also on the social relationship surrounding the purchasing decision.

02 — The issue was not just accuracy. It was also relational meaning.

Consumers evaluated AI gift recommendations through two distinct motivations: relational signalling and preference matching.

Study 2 investigated why consumers responded differently to AI recommendations depending on relationship closeness.

Two psychological motivations emerged:

Relational Signalling

Whether using AI helps the gift giver communicate the meaning of their relationship with the recipient.

Preference Matching

Whether using AI helps the giver identify a gift that matches the recipient's preferences.

Both were rated lower when participants considered AI recommendations for close friends rather than distant friends.

Mediation analysis identified significant indirect effects through both motivations.

These findings suggest that consumers may hesitate to use AI for close friends because they question both the tool's ability to reflect the relationship and its ability to capture the recipient's personal preferences.

Key insight: AI recommendation systems need to address emotional and relational expectations, not simply product suitability.

03 — Context and AI design can change the experience

Consumers' receptivity to AI recommendations depends on who they are buying for, the gift-giving context, and how the AI tool interacts with them.

Three additional experiments examined important boundary conditions.

Self-Oriented Perfectionism — Study 3

Gift givers with higher self-oriented perfectionism showed a stronger distinction between close and distant friends in their expectations of AI's preference-matching ability.

Those with high perfectionism were particularly doubtful that AI could recommend suitably personalised gifts for close friends.

Gift-Giver Identity — Study 4

In a Secret Santa context, whether the giver's identity was disclosed influenced relational signalling and preference-matching expectations.

For close colleagues, keeping the giver's identity undisclosed was associated with more favourable expectations of using AI for gift selection.

Conversational Turn-Taking — Study 5

A conversational AI tool that asked questions sequentially improved expected preference matching when participants selected gifts for close friends.

The same improvement was not observed for distant friends.

These studies identified changes in the underlying psychological pathways; they did not establish that the moderators significantly changed the overall social-closeness effect on AI usage intention.

Key insight: More conversational and personalised AI experiences may be particularly valuable for consumers choosing gifts for people with whom they have close relationships.

Recommendations

Research-informed product directions. The studies measured intentions and expectations; these recommendations do not imply a validated increase in real-world adoption or sales.

01 — Segment AI Gift Recommendations by Relationship Closeness

Gift recommendation platforms should consider the giver–recipient relationship alongside conventional inputs such as age, interests, and occasion.

For acquaintances and distant friends, AI tools can emphasise efficiency, convenience, and broadly suitable gift suggestions.

For close relationships, recommendation experiences may require greater attention to personal meaning, shared experiences, and individual preferences.

02 — Design More Personalised, Conversational Experiences

Introduce conversational turn-taking that allows gift givers to provide recipient information progressively.

Instead of relying only on a single questionnaire, AI tools can ask follow-up questions about personal interests, hobbies, memories, and preferences.

The research suggests that this interaction style can improve expected preference matching, particularly when choosing gifts for close friends.

03 — Align AI Recommendations With Gift-Giving Contexts

Different gift-giving occasions involve different social expectations.

AI recommendation tools can adapt their positioning to contexts such as Secret Santa, workplace exchanges, weddings, and birthdays.

For gift-giving occasions involving anonymity or distant relationships, marketers may emphasise convenience and gift suitability. For closer relationships, greater personalisation and sensitivity to relational meaning may be more relevant.
