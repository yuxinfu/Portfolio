AI-generated review summaries · Step 4 of 5

Analysis

From observed contributions to psychological mechanisms: what the evidence shows, and where interpretation needs care.

The analytical story

AI summaries make reviews easier to consume. The evidence points to a cost for contribution—and a role for preserving recognition.

01From Data to Evidence

The analysis asks three connected questions: do AI-generated review summaries change contributions, what psychological process is consistent with that change, and can platform design help?

Observe

Real-world contributions

Matched hotels on TripAdvisor and Expedia, analysed with difference-in-differences.

Explain

Recognition and motivation

Randomized service and product experiments test review-writing intentions and anticipated recognition.

Intervene

Visible reader engagement

A factorial experiment examines engagement cues and conditional indirect effects.

Evidence basis: Study 1’s supplied replication report; independently recalculated means and group comparisons for Studies 2a, 2b, and 3; and the saved SPSS PROCESS outputs for bootstrap intervals. The field models and bootstrap analyses have not been rerun for this page.

02Field Evidence: Contributions Declined

Study 1 compares the change in review activity on TripAdvisor after October 25, 2023 with the corresponding change on Expedia for the same hotels. The primary panel contains 188 hotels and 117,312 hotel × platform × day observations.

What the model compares

Outcome = β(TripAdvisor × Post) + hotel × platform FE + city × day FE + error

Hotel × platform fixed effects absorb stable differences within each listing. City × day fixed effects absorb shocks shared by hotels in the same city on the same day. Standard errors are clustered by hotel.

Interpretation: the estimated relative decline is consistent with summaries crowding out contribution. Identification still depends on parallel trends and the absence of relevant spillovers or unaccounted platform-specific changes.

03Experiments: Lower Willingness and Writing Effort

The service experiment (Study 2a, n = 402) and product experiment (Study 2b, n = 399) compare randomized AI-summary and control conditions. Both show lower willingness to leave a review and lower intended writing effort in the AI-summary condition.

04Mechanism: Anticipated Recognition

Evidence consistent with a mechanism

The indirect effects support anticipated recognition as an explanation consistent with the data. Because the mediator itself was measured rather than randomized, these estimates do not independently establish causal mediation.

05Reader Engagement: A More Qualified Intervention Result

Study 3 (n = 624) crosses AI-summary presence with highlighted reader engagement. The summary-related gaps are smaller when engagement is highlighted, but the statistical evidence differs by outcome.

The strongest intervention evidence concerns recognition and the indirect pathway. A significant comparison in one condition and a nonsignificant comparison in another does not, by itself, establish an interaction. The direct willingness and effort interactions should therefore not be presented as conclusive.

Negative estimates persist under weekly aggregation and broader sample definitions. The placebo estimates are not significant at 10%. The triple-difference evidence is weaker for review volume (10% level) than for text production (5% level).

The archive also contains eight supplementary-study folders. Their presence documents a broader research programme; the table above reports the field robustness results verified for this page and does not imply that every supplementary analysis has been independently reproduced.

Supported by these analyses

  • Lower relative field contribution after AI-summary introduction.
  • Lower willingness and intended effort in two randomized contexts.
  • Negative indirect associations through anticipated recognition.
  • Engagement moderates the recognition pathway.

Not established here

  • A universal effect across all platforms or populations.
  • Definitive causal mediation.
  • Significant direct engagement interactions on both behavioural intentions.
  • Sales, profit, or implementation ROI.
