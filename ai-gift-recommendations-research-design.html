Research Design

AI Gift Recommendations & Consumer Adoption · Step 3 of 5

From theory to experimentation: examining how social closeness, consumer motivations, gift-giving contexts, and conversational AI design shape consumers' willingness to use AI gift recommendation tools.

01 / INTRODUCTION TO RESEARCH DESIGN

Translating Consumer Psychology into Experimental Evidence

This research employed a quantitative experimental approach comprising five studies with 1,293 participants.

The experiments examined whether consumers' willingness to use AI gift recommendation tools depends on the relationship between the gift giver and recipient.

Rather than focusing on the technical accuracy of AI recommendations, the research investigated how consumers expect AI to support two important gift-giving motivations: relational signaling and preference matching.

The five studies progressively explored the main relationship, its psychological mechanisms, and three boundary conditions.

4 Gift-Giving Contexts

Birthdays, weddings, Secret Santa, and housewarming parties.

3 Boundary Conditions

Self-oriented perfectionism, identity disclosure, and AI conversational turn-taking.

The research programme moves from identifying differences in consumer adoption to examining the psychological motivations and contextual factors behind those differences.

02 / RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

Quantitative Experimental Approach

The studies used scenario-based between-subjects experiments to investigate consumers' responses to AI gift recommendation tools.

Participants were assigned to gift-giving scenarios involving different levels of social closeness.

Depending on the study, additional variables were measured or manipulated to investigate factors that might change consumers' expectations of AI.

Independent Variable: Social Closeness

Across the five experiments, the central comparison involved two types of relationships.

Close Friend

A recipient with whom the giver shares a close personal relationship.

Hi-Bye Friend

A more distant friend or acquaintance with whom the giver has a less intimate relationship.

By varying the type of relationship, the experiments examined whether the same AI recommendation tool would be evaluated differently depending on the intended recipient.

Dependent Variable: AI Usage Intention

The primary outcome was participants' self-reported likelihood of using an AI gift recommendation tool when selecting a gift.

The measure used a ten-point response scale, with higher scores indicating greater willingness to use AI assistance.

Psychological Mediators

Two motivations were examined:

Expected Relational Signaling

The extent to which participants believed that using an AI gift recommendation tool would help express or acknowledge their relationship with the recipient.

Expected Preference Matching

The extent to which participants believed that using AI would help identify a gift matching the recipient's preferences.

Both were measured using seven-point response scales.

Boundary Conditions

The research considered three factors:

  • Self-oriented perfectionism, measured as a continuous individual characteristic.
  • Disclosure of the gift giver's identity, experimentally manipulated in Study 4.
  • AI conversational turn-taking, experimentally manipulated in Study 5.

These variables were investigated to understand when the psychological motivations underlying AI adoption might differ.

03 / EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN

Study 1 — Establishing the Effect of Social Closeness

Research objective

Examine whether consumers are less willing to use AI gift recommendation tools when choosing gifts for close friends compared with hi-bye friends.

Experimental design

A single-factor, two-condition between-subjects experiment.

Gift-giving context: Birthday.

Participants imagined shopping for a birthday gift for either a close friend or a hi-bye friend.

They were informed that a shopping website they normally used had introduced an AI-powered gift recommendation tool.

Participants viewed a visual representation of the recommendation interface, modelled on an existing gift recommendation service, before evaluating their likelihood of using it.

Experimental Conditions

Condition ACondition B
Birthday gift for a close friendBirthday gift for a hi-bye friend
Close social relationshipDistant social relationship
Same AI recommendation conceptSame AI recommendation concept

Sample

  • Recruited: 216 participants.
  • Final sample: 209 participants.
  • Recruitment: Prolific, United Kingdom.
  • Experimental conditions: 2.

Measurement

The principal outcome was likelihood of using the AI recommendation tool on a ten-point scale.

A manipulation check assessed whether participants correctly identified the intended recipient relationship.

Analytical approach

Linear regression with social closeness as the independent variable and AI usage intention as the dependent variable. Age and gender were included as covariates.

Design contribution

Study 1 established an initial experimental test of whether relationship closeness is relevant to AI recommendation adoption.

Study 2 — Investigating the Dual Motivations

Research objective

Examine whether expected relational signaling and expected preference matching help explain the relationship between social closeness and willingness to use AI gift recommendations.

Experimental design

A single-factor, two-condition between-subjects experiment using the birthday gift scenario.

The same social-closeness manipulation was retained, while additional measurements assessed the two proposed psychological motivations.

Experimental Conditions

Close friendHi-bye friend
AI usage intention measuredAI usage intention measured
Both psychological motivations measuredBoth psychological motivations measured

Sample

  • Recruited: 300 participants.
  • Final sample: 269 participants.
  • Recruitment: Prolific, United Kingdom.
  • Experimental conditions: 2.

Key measurements

  • Likelihood of using the AI gift recommendation tool.
  • Expected relational signaling.
  • Expected preference matching.
  • Social-closeness manipulation check.

Analytical approach

Parallel mediation analysis using PROCESS Model 4 with 5,000 bootstrap samples.

Social closeness was the independent variable, the two psychological motivations were entered as mediators, and likelihood of using AI was the dependent variable.

Age and gender were included as covariates.

Design contribution

Study 2 extended the investigation beyond whether relationship closeness matters to examine why consumers may respond differently to AI-assisted gift selection.

Study 3 — Self-Oriented Perfectionism

Research objective

Examine whether self-oriented perfectionism changes the relationship between social closeness, expected preference matching, and AI usage intention.

Experimental design

A scenario-based between-subjects experiment in which social closeness was manipulated and self-oriented perfectionism was measured.

Gift-giving context: Wedding.

Participants imagined receiving a wedding invitation from either a close friend or a hi-bye friend.

They then evaluated the use of an AI gift recommendation tool and completed measures of the proposed psychological motivations and self-oriented perfectionism.

Research Variables

VariableOperationalisation
Social closenessClose friend vs. hi-bye friend
Self-oriented perfectionismFive-item individual-difference measure

Expected preference matchingSeven-point measure

Sample

  • Recruited: 300 participants.
  • Final sample: 280 participants.
  • Recruitment: Prolific, United Kingdom.

Self-Oriented Perfectionism Measurement

The five-item measure assessed the extent to which participants held demanding personal standards when selecting gifts.

Responses were averaged to create a self-oriented perfectionism index.

The scale demonstrated good internal consistency (Cronbach's α = .88).

Analytical approach

Moderated mediation analysis using PROCESS Model 8 with 5,000 bootstrap samples.

The model examined whether self-oriented perfectionism moderated the relationships involving social closeness and the psychological mediators, as well as AI usage intention.

Age and gender were included as covariates.

Design contribution

Study 3 extended the investigation to a different gift-giving occasion and examined how an individual consumer characteristic may influence expectations of AI recommendation quality.

Study 4 — Gift-Giver Identity Disclosure

Research objective

Examine whether disclosing the gift giver's identity changes the effects of social closeness on expected relational signaling and AI usage intention.

Experimental design

A 2 × 2 between-subjects factorial experiment.

Gift-giving context: Workplace Secret Santa.

The design manipulated two factors:

  • Social closeness: close vs. hi-bye colleague.
  • Identity disclosure: disclosed vs. undisclosed.

Four Experimental Conditions

Identity DisclosedIdentity Undisclosed
Close colleagueClose relationship, identifiable giverClose relationship, anonymous giver
Hi-bye colleagueDistant relationship, identifiable giverDistant relationship, anonymous giver

In the disclosure condition, the scenario described a gift exchange in which participants' identities could be revealed.

In the non-disclosure condition, gift givers remained anonymous.

This manipulation created different opportunities for the recipient to connect a gift with the giver's identity and relationship.

Sample

  • Recruited: 300 participants.
  • Final sample: 256 participants.
  • Recruitment: Prolific, United Kingdom.
  • Experimental conditions: 4.

Key measurements

  • Likelihood of using AI gift recommendations.
  • Expected relational signaling.
  • Expected preference matching.
  • Social closeness.
  • Identity-disclosure manipulation check.

Analytical approach

Moderated mediation analysis using PROCESS Model 8 with 5,000 bootstrap samples.

Social closeness was the independent variable, identity disclosure was the moderator, and expected relational signaling and preference matching served as mediators.

AI usage intention was the dependent variable.

Age and gender were included as covariates.

Design contribution

Study 4 examined whether the social visibility of the gift giver's identity changes how consumers evaluate AI assistance.

It provides an empirical basis for considering differences between anonymous and identified gift-giving occasions.

Study 5 — Conversational Turn-Taking

Research objective

Examine whether conversational turn-taking changes gift givers' expectations of AI's ability to identify suitable gifts, particularly for close friends.

Experimental design

A 2 × 2 between-subjects factorial experiment.

Gift-giving context: Housewarming.

The study manipulated:

  • Social closeness: close friend vs. hi-bye friend.
  • AI turn-taking ability: present vs. absent.

Four Experimental Conditions

Turn-Taking PresentTurn-Taking Absent
Close friendSequential AI questionsAll AI questions presented together
Hi-bye friendSequential AI questionsAll AI questions presented together

Interface Manipulation

In the turn-taking condition, the AI presented recipient-related questions sequentially.

After participants answered one question, the next question appeared.

In the no-turn-taking condition, participants viewed all questions simultaneously.

Both interfaces used the same eight questions, covering recipient demographics, interests, and hobbies.

This design enabled the researchers to investigate the effect of conversational interaction while retaining the same information-gathering content.

Sample

  • Recruited: 300 participants.
  • Final sample: 279 participants.
  • Recruitment: Prolific, United Kingdom.
  • Experimental conditions: 4.

Key measurements

  • Likelihood of using AI gift recommendations.
  • Expected preference matching.
  • Expected relational signaling.
  • Social closeness.
  • Perceived conversational turn-taking ability.

Manipulation Validation

Participants completed a three-item social-closeness measure and a measure assessing whether the AI interface was perceived as supporting conversational turn-taking.

The turn-taking measure demonstrated good internal consistency (Cronbach's α = .89).

Analytical approach

Moderated mediation analysis using PROCESS Model 8 with 5,000 bootstrap samples.

The analysis examined whether turn-taking changed the relationship between social closeness and the psychological motivations underlying AI usage intention.

Age and gender were included as covariates.

Design contribution

Study 5 moved beyond consumer and contextual characteristics to examine a feature that AI recommendation companies can potentially modify in product design.

04 / SAMPLING STRATEGY

Participant Recruitment

Participants were recruited through Prolific, with the published studies using United Kingdom samples.

Respondents were assigned to the experimental conditions within each study.

The research restricted participants from taking part in more than one of the five experiments to minimise repeated-exposure effects.

Exclusion Procedures

The published research reports exclusion of participants who failed the relevant attention or manipulation checks.

Study 1 excluded seven participants who failed the social-closeness manipulation check.

Study 2 excluded 31 participants who failed the manipulation check.

Study 3 excluded 20 participants who failed attention or manipulation checks.

Study 4 excluded 44 participants who failed attention or manipulation checks.

Study 5 excluded 21 participants who failed an attention check.

Sampling Considerations

Consistency

All five studies used the same online recruitment platform, supporting consistency in the participant recruitment approach.

Independent Samples

Previous study participants were blocked from participating again, helping to avoid data contamination across experiments.

Contextual Breadth

The research tested consumer responses across four familiar gift-giving occasions.

Generalisation

Although the experiments provide evidence about responses within the tested samples and scenarios, the participants were not recruited to represent every national market or cultural context.

05 / QUESTIONNAIRE DESIGN

Translating Psychological Constructs into Measures

The questionnaires were designed to capture participants' intentions to use AI gift recommendation tools and their expectations of AI's ability to support different gift-giving motivations.

The central outcome and psychological measures were kept consistent across studies wherever applicable.

Measurement Preview

A. Likelihood of Using AI

Participants indicated how likely they would be to use an AI gift recommendation tool to select a gift for the recipient described in their scenario.

The measure used a ten-point scale.

Purpose: Capture consumer adoption intention.

B. Expected Relational Signaling

Participants evaluated whether using the AI gift recommendation tool would help acknowledge or express their relationship with the recipient.

The measure used a seven-point agreement scale.

Purpose: Capture perceived relational value.

C. Expected Preference Matching

Participants evaluated whether the AI gift recommendation tool would help identify a gift suited to the recipient's preferences.

The measure used a seven-point agreement scale.

Purpose: Capture perceived recommendation suitability.

D. Self-Oriented Perfectionism

Study 3 included five items measuring the participant's personal standards when selecting gifts.

The items were combined into a self-oriented perfectionism index.

Purpose: Examine whether demanding standards influence consumer expectations of AI.

E. Identity Disclosure

Study 4 included a manipulation check assessing whether participants understood that their identity would or would not be revealed in the Secret Santa exchange.

Purpose: Validate the identity-disclosure manipulation.

F. Conversational Turn-Taking

Study 5 assessed whether participants perceived the AI interface as supporting reciprocal, sequential interaction.

Purpose: Validate the experimental difference between sequential and simultaneous question presentation.

Questionnaire Design Principles

Comparable Measures

Consistent use of the principal outcome and psychological measures supported comparisons across the five studies.

Contextual Scenarios

Each experiment used a gift-giving occasion to help participants evaluate AI assistance within a social situation.

Experimental Manipulations

Social closeness, identity disclosure, and turn-taking were manipulated in their respective experimental designs.

Psychological Measurement

Expected relational signaling, expected preference matching, and self-oriented perfectionism were measured rather than experimentally manipulated.

Manipulation Checks

Study-specific checks assessed whether participants correctly understood the relevant experimental conditions.

06 / RESEARCH QUALITY AND LIMITATIONS

Internal Validity

The experiments used random assignment to conditions and manipulation checks to assess whether the intended differences were understood.

Across the research programme, the same central social-closeness distinction was examined in different scenarios.

This enabled the researchers to investigate whether the proposed relationships extended beyond a single gift-giving occasion.

Analytical Strategy

Three types of analysis were used:

Regression Analysis

Study 1 tested the relationship between social closeness and AI usage intention while including age and gender as covariates.

Mediation Analysis

Study 2 used PROCESS Model 4 with 5,000 bootstrap samples to examine expected relational signaling and preference matching as mediators.

Moderated Mediation Analysis

Studies 3, 4, and 5 used PROCESS Model 8 with 5,000 bootstrap samples to examine how the proposed pathways varied with perfectionism, identity disclosure, and turn-taking.

The models included age and gender as covariates.

Research Quality Measures

The published paper reports several procedures applied across the studies:

  • Sample-size targets were established before data collection using sample sizes from previous gift-giving experiments as reference points.
  • All measured variables were reported.
  • Participants who failed specified attention or manipulation checks were excluded.
  • Analyses without covariates produced similar effect directions and significance conclusions.
  • Participants were prevented from joining multiple studies.

Design Limitations

Hypothetical Gift-Giving Decisions

The studies used experimental scenarios rather than tracking real gift purchases.

Self-Reported Adoption Intention

The primary outcome measured consumers' likelihood of using AI, not verified adoption or subsequent purchasing behaviour.

Single-Item Core Measures

AI usage intention, expected relational signaling, and expected preference matching were each assessed using single-item measures. Their internal consistency cannot be evaluated in the same way as that of multi-item scales.

Measured Psychological Mediators

The mediators were measured rather than independently randomised. The indirect-effect analyses therefore support the proposed psychological explanations but do not, by themselves, establish definitive causal mediation.

Limited Market Representation

The samples were drawn from United Kingdom respondents recruited through Prolific. Further research would be needed to establish generalisability to other markets and cultures.

Interface Design Scope

Study 5 compared sequential versus simultaneous presentation of the same eight questions. It did not test every possible conversational AI design, adaptive recommendation algorithm, or actual recommendation accuracy.

07 / RESEARCH DESIGN SUMMARY

Five Experiments, One Research Programme

The five studies followed a progressive research strategy.

Study 1 — Establish

Test whether social closeness influences willingness to use AI gift recommendation tools.

Study 2 — Explain

Investigate relational signaling and preference matching as the proposed psychological mechanisms.

Study 3 — Extend

Examine how self-oriented perfectionism influences the proposed relationships.

Study 4 — Contextualise

Investigate the role of gift-giver identity disclosure in a Secret Santa exchange.

Study 5 — Apply to Design

Test whether conversational turn-taking changes consumers' expectations of AI's ability to recommend suitable gifts.

Together, these studies connect consumer psychology with research questions relevant to AI recommendation design and marketing.

The programme moves beyond asking whether consumers accept AI to investigate how relationship closeness, psychological motivations, and interface characteristics contribute to their adoption intentions.
