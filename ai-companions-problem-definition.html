Problem Definition

AI companionship & consumer aversion · Step 1 of 5

Understanding how the growing social role of AI creates unintended consequences for consumer privacy perceptions and AI acceptance.

Central business question

How can AI companion companies promote emotionally engaging experiences while addressing privacy concerns among consumers who observe, rather than directly use, these technologies?

01 / CONTEXT

AI Companionship: Business Context and Research Purpose

Business Context

Artificial intelligence is evolving beyond its traditional role as a functional assistant. AI-powered applications are increasingly designed to provide emotional support, companionship, and even romantic interactions.

For companies developing these products, emotional engagement represents an important aspect of the consumer experience. However, these experiences do not exist in isolation.

Consumers may encounter stories of human–AI relationships through advertising, social media, news coverage, or conversations with others, even if they have never used an AI companion themselves.

This creates a potential business challenge: AI products that appeal to their intended users may generate negative responses among people who observe how others interact with them.

Two Consumer Perspectives

Direct AI Users

People who interact with AI for functional assistance, emotional support, or companionship.

Business consideration: Product engagement and user experience.

Third-Party Observers

People who encounter others' interactions with AI through social communication, advertising, or news.

Business consideration: Privacy perceptions and willingness to engage with AI.

Why This Matters

AI companies often evaluate product success through direct user experiences, including satisfaction, engagement, and adoption.

However, AI companionship introduces a broader social dimension. As human–AI relationships become publicly visible, consumers may evaluate whether these interactions fit their expectations of AI's appropriate role.

When AI appears to cross established social boundaries, observers may become uncertain about their own ability to understand, predict, or control AI systems.

For businesses, this raises an important question: Can the way people use AI influence how others evaluate the technology, even without direct product experience?

Research Purpose

This project investigates how observing human–AI interactions that violate established social norms influences consumers' aversion to AI.

Specifically, the research aims to:

  • Examine whether observing emotionally intimate human–AI interactions increases privacy concern and reduces willingness to share personal data with AI.
  • Investigate whether AI-related perceived control helps explain these responses.
  • Examine whether perceived AI autonomy changes the psychological pathway linking observed norm violation to AI aversion.

02 / PROBLEM FRAMING

From Business Problem to Research Problem

The business challenge can be translated into two distinct questions: the decision AI product teams need to make, and the empirical evidence needed to inform that decision.

Management Decision Problem

How should AI companion companies position and communicate relational AI products while accounting for the responses of audiences beyond their direct users?

This decision involves balancing the appeal of emotionally engaging AI services with how these services are interpreted by consumers who encounter them indirectly.

It has implications for product positioning, marketing communication, and the presentation of AI's capabilities and boundaries.

Marketing Research Problem

Determine whether observing human–AI interactions that are high, rather than low, in perceived norm violation influences consumers' privacy concern and willingness to share personal data.

Investigate whether AI-related perceived control accounts for part of this relationship and whether the strength of this indirect pathway depends on how autonomous the AI is perceived to be.

The research translates the broader business challenge into measurable consumer perceptions and intentions.

Decision Framework

01 — Business Situation

AI companionship is becoming increasingly visible through public communication, marketing, and media coverage.

02 — Management Decision

How should AI companion products be positioned and communicated to audiences with different expectations about AI's social role?

Perceived norm violation, privacy concern, willingness to share personal data, AI-related perceived control, and perceived AI autonomy.

04 — Business Application

Inform research-based considerations for AI product positioning, communication strategies, and the presentation of user control.

04 / RESEARCH PRIORITIES

Four Questions Guide the Research

Four priorities define the research problem.

1. Observed Social Norm Violation

Examine whether observing human–AI interactions that depart from conventional expectations increases consumer aversion towards AI.

In particular, compare reactions to AI used as a functional assistant with AI involved in emotionally intimate or romantic relationships.

Key question: Does observing unconventional human–AI interaction influence consumers' privacy concern and willingness to share personal data?

2. AI-Related Perceived Control

Investigate the psychological mechanism through which observing norm-violating human–AI interactions may influence consumer responses.

The research considers whether these interactions undermine observers' sense that AI is predictable and manageable, thereby contributing to greater AI aversion.

Key question: Does reduced AI-related perceived control help explain why observers become more averse to AI?

3. The Role of AI Autonomy

Examine whether perceived AI autonomy changes how observers interpret unconventional human–AI relationships.

The research distinguishes between AI described as following human instructions and AI described as capable of acting independently.

Key question: Does perceived AI autonomy change the indirect relationship between observed norm violation and AI aversion through perceived control?

4. Exposure Contexts and Consumer Responses

Examine the relationship across different ways consumers encounter human–AI interactions.

The three experiments use interpersonal communication, hypothetical advertising, and hypothetical news coverage to examine responses across different exposure contexts.

Key question: Do similar patterns of AI aversion emerge across different scenarios in which consumers observe others' AI use?

05 / SCOPE

Scope and Boundaries

In Scope

  • Third-party observers' responses to human–AI interactions.
  • Conventional AI assistance versus emotionally intimate or romantic AI use.
  • Privacy concern and willingness to share personal data.
  • AI-related perceived control as a proposed psychological mechanism.
  • Perceived AI autonomy as a boundary condition.
  • Evidence from three randomised, scenario-based experiments.

Out of Scope

  • Actual usage behaviour of AI companion applications.
  • Observed disclosure of personal data to real AI products.
  • Technical evaluation of AI system safety or cybersecurity.
  • Direct sales, conversion, retention, or financial ROI.
  • Long-term changes in consumer attitudes following repeated exposure.
  • Direct testing of product redesigns or marketing interventions.
  • A comprehensive evaluation of all forms of human–AI relationships.

Interpretation Boundary

The research measures privacy concern and self-reported willingness to share personal data, rather than actual data-sharing behaviour.

Random assignment supports causal inference about exposure conditions and measured responses. Mediation analysis examines evidence consistent with a proposed psychological mechanism; it does not independently establish definitive causal mediation.

The research distinguishes moderation of the indirect pathway through perceived control from moderation of overall consumer responses. These are separate empirical questions; one should not be inferred from the other.

These boundaries are important when translating experimental findings into potential business recommendations.

06 / WHAT COMES NEXT

How Problem Definition Connects to the Research Process

01 — Problem Definition

Identify the business challenge created by socially visible AI companionship, distinguish managerial decisions from research questions, and establish the research priorities.

02 — Research Approach

Develop the theoretical framework using social norms and Compensatory Control Theory, and specify the proposed relationships between norm violation, perceived control, AI aversion, and AI autonomy.

03 — Research Design

Describe the three randomised experiments, interpersonal and media exposure scenarios, participant samples, measurement scales, and hypotheses.

04 — Analysis

Examine the effects of observed norm violation on privacy concern and willingness to share personal data, test mediation through perceived control, and evaluate the moderating role of AI autonomy.

05 — Report: Insights and Implications

Translate the experimental evidence into research-informed considerations for AI companion positioning, marketing communication, privacy, and user control.

With the business challenge and research priorities established, Step 2 develops the theoretical approach explaining why observing others' intimate relationships with AI may increase consumer aversion.
