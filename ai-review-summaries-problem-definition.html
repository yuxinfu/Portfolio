AI-generated review summaries · Step 1 of 5

Problem Definition

Defining the hidden trade-off between AI-powered review consumption and consumer-generated content.

Central business question

How can online review platforms improve the reading experience with AI without undermining consumers' motivation to contribute reviews?

01 / CONTEXTPlatform Context and Research Purpose

Business Context

Online review platforms increasingly use AI-generated summaries (AIGS) to condense large volumes of consumer feedback into concise, accessible overviews.

These summaries help consumers process information more efficiently and make purchasing decisions with less effort. However, review platforms depend on consumers not only to read reviews, but also to continuously contribute new ones.

This creates a potential challenge: A feature designed to improve how consumers read reviews may inadvertently discourage them from writing reviews.

Why This Matters

The long-term value of online review platforms depends on maintaining a sustainable flow of authentic consumer-generated content. If contributors expect their individual reviews to receive less attention because readers increasingly rely on AI summaries, their motivation to contribute may decline.

Research Purpose

This project investigates whether the introduction of AI-generated review summaries affects consumers' willingness to write reviews and the effort they invest in doing so. It also examines the psychological mechanisms underlying these responses and explores how platforms might mitigate potentially negative consequences.

02 / PROBLEM FRAMINGFrom Business Problem to Research Problem

Platform Management Decision Problem

How should online review platforms integrate AI-generated summaries while maintaining consumers' motivation to contribute original reviews?

This decision involves balancing the information-processing benefits of AI summaries with the need to preserve active consumer participation.

Marketing Research Problem

Determine whether the presence of AI-generated review summaries influences review-writing behaviour, identify the psychological mechanism underlying this relationship, and examine potential platform design strategies that could mitigate negative effects.

The research translates the broader platform challenge into observable and measurable consumer responses.

Decision Framework

  1. Business Situation

    AI-generated summaries make large volumes of reviews easier to navigate.

  2. Platform Decision

    How can platforms retain the benefits of AI summaries without discouraging review contributions?

  3. Research Evidence

    Review-writing willingness, writing effort, anticipated recognition, and reader-engagement cues.

  4. Business Application

    Inform the design and implementation of AI-powered review features while supporting ongoing consumer participation.

04 / RESEARCH PRIORITIESFour Questions Guide the Research

Four priorities define the research problem.

  1. Review Contribution

    Examine whether the availability of AI-generated summaries influences consumers' willingness to leave online reviews and the volume of reviews contributed.

    Key question: Are consumers less inclined to contribute when AI summaries are available?

  2. Review-Writing Effort

    Assess whether AI-generated summaries influence the amount of time, thought, and effort consumers are willing to invest in writing reviews.

    Key question: Does the presence of AI summaries change how much effort consumers put into individual reviews?

  3. Anticipated Recognition

    Investigate whether consumers expect their individual contributions to receive less attention, appreciation, or acknowledgment when AI summaries are present.

    Key question: Does anticipated social recognition help explain changes in review-writing behaviour?

  4. Platform Design and Reader Engagement

    Examine whether making reader engagement more visible, through features such as helpful votes and comments, can reduce potential negative effects on review contribution.

    Key question: Can visible acknowledgment of individual reviews help sustain consumer participation?

05 / SCOPEScope and Boundaries

In Scope

  • AI-generated summaries of online consumer reviews.
  • Consumers' willingness to leave reviews.
  • Review-writing effort and contribution volume.
  • Anticipated recognition as a proposed psychological mechanism.
  • Reader-engagement indicators as a potential mitigating design feature.
  • Evidence from real-world platform activity and controlled experiments.

Out of Scope

  • Direct financial return on AI summary implementation.
  • Sales attribution and purchase-conversion modelling.
  • Long-term platform profitability or customer lifetime value.
  • All forms of generative AI beyond review summarisation.
  • A comprehensive evaluation of every possible motivation for review writing.

Interpretation Boundary

The research focuses on review contribution and its proposed psychological drivers. Experimental indirect effects provide evidence consistent with the proposed mechanism but do not, on their own, establish definitive causal mediation. Field estimates also depend on the assumptions of the research design.
