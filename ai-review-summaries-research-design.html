Research Design

From research framework to data collection: combining real-world platform behaviour with controlled experiments.

01Introduction to Research Design

This research employs a multi-method quantitative design combining a quasi-experimental field study with three preregistered online experiments.

The field study uses actual consumer review data from TripAdvisor and Expedia to investigate whether the introduction of AI-generated review summaries (AIGS) affects review contribution behaviour.

The experimental studies complement this observational evidence by manipulating the presence of AI summaries in controlled review environments. They investigate whether AI summaries affect consumers' willingness to leave reviews, the effort they intend to invest in writing, and their anticipated recognition from other readers.

A final experiment examines whether highlighting reader engagement can mitigate these effects.

02Research Methodology

Study 1 — Quasi-Experimental Field Study

Research objective

Determine whether introducing AI-generated review summaries changes the quantity of consumer reviews and the amount of text produced.

Natural Experiment

On October 25, 2023, TripAdvisor introduced AI-generated review summaries on eligible hotel pages.

Expedia, which features reviews of many of the same hotels, did not introduce its own AI summaries until May 2024.

This difference in implementation timing creates an opportunity to compare review-writing activity across platforms before and after TripAdvisor introduced the feature.

Study Design

Treatment Platform: TripAdvisor

AI-generated summaries were introduced on eligible hotel pages on October 25, 2023.

Comparison Platform: Expedia

The same hotels were observed on a platform that had not yet introduced AI-generated summaries during the study window.

Observation Period

July 7, 2023 – May 13, 2024

  • Pre-intervention: 110 days
  • Post-intervention: 202 days

Analytical Approach

A difference-in-differences (DID) design compares changes in review activity on TripAdvisor against corresponding changes on Expedia for matched hotels.

Using the same hotels across both platforms helps account for hotel-specific conditions that may affect consumer reviewing, such as seasonal demand, local events, or renovations.

Dataset Construction

The research collected review histories from hotels in five U.S. leisure destinations:

  • Santa Barbara, California
  • Scottsdale, Arizona
  • Charleston, South Carolina
  • Savannah, Georgia
  • San Antonio, Texas

Hotel listings were matched across the two platforms using hotel names, geographic locations, street addresses, and hotel characteristics.

The full matching process identified 723 verified hotel pairs.

The primary analytical sample was constructed by retaining hotels with at least 30 English-language TripAdvisor reviews during the 12 months preceding the observation window.

Key Variables

Independent Variable

The interaction between the TripAdvisor platform indicator and the post-introduction period.

Dependent Variables

  1. Review volume: log(1 + number of text reviews posted per hotel, platform, and day).
  2. Text production: log(1 + total review words posted per hotel, platform, and day).

Model Controls

  • Hotel × platform fixed effects.
  • City × day fixed effects.
  • Standard errors clustered at the hotel level.

Research Design Logic

The analysis focuses on whether the gap in review contributions between TripAdvisor and Expedia changes following the introduction of AI summaries.

The design also includes event-time analysis and robustness checks to evaluate the stability of the estimated relationship.

03Experimental Design

To complement real-world observational evidence, the project uses controlled online experiments to examine the psychological drivers of review-writing behaviour.

Participants were recruited through Prolific and completed the studies using Qualtrics.

Study 2a — Service Context

Research objective

Examine whether the presence of AI-generated summaries affects willingness to leave reviews, intended writing effort, and anticipated recognition.

Scenario

Participants were asked to imagine that they had recently visited a restaurant and were considering leaving an online review.

They were randomly assigned to one of two versions of a review webpage.

Control GroupExperimental Group
Human-written customer reviewsAI-generated summary plus individual customer reviews
The first review was attributed to a consumerThe same information was presented as an AI-generated summary
No AI summary was displayedThe AI summary appeared prominently above individual reviews

Importantly, the content of the first review was held constant across conditions. The key manipulation was whether the information was presented as an individual human review or an AI-generated summary.

Final sample: N = 402

Key outcomes

  • Willingness to leave a review.
  • Intended writing effort.
  • Anticipated review helpfulness.
  • Anticipated social recognition.

This design tests whether the presentation of review information affects potential contributors' expectations and behavioural intentions.

Study 2b — Product Context

Research objective

Examine whether the relationships investigated in Study 2a extend beyond service experiences to physical products.

Study 2b replicated the basic experimental design of Study 2a, replacing the restaurant scenario with a coffee machine.

Participants again encountered either individual human-written reviews or an AI-generated summary presented alongside individual reviews.

Final sample: N = 399

Design: AI summary present versus absent.

Measurements: The same principal review-writing and recognition measures as Study 2a.

Using a physical product provides an opportunity to examine whether the proposed effects are consistent across service and product review contexts.

Study 3 — Reader Engagement Intervention

Research objective

Evaluate whether highlighting engagement with individual reviews can mitigate the effects associated with AI-generated summaries.

Scenario

Participants imagined visiting a coffee shop and considering leaving an online review.

Experimental Design

A 2 × 2 between-subjects factorial design:

  • Factor 1: AI-generated summary present versus absent.
  • Factor 2: Highlighted reader engagement present versus absent.

Four Experimental Conditions

AI summary conditionNo Engagement HighlightEngagement Highlight
No AI SummaryCondition 1: Human reviews without highlighted reader engagementCondition 2: Human reviews with highlighted reader engagement
AI SummaryCondition 3: AI summary without highlighted reader engagementCondition 4: AI summary with highlighted reader engagement

Reader Engagement Manipulation

In the engagement-highlight conditions, participants were informed that the platform encouraged interaction with individual customer reviews.

The scenario featured visible reader-response indicators, including an example of a review receiving 568 top-comment votes and another receiving more than 100 comments.

These numbers were elements of the experimental scenario, not observed platform engagement statistics.

Final sample: N = 624

Key outcomes

  • Willingness to leave a review.
  • Intended writing effort.
  • Anticipated review helpfulness.

The study evaluates whether highlighting reader engagement changes the relationship between AI summaries and anticipated recognition, as well as the associated review-writing outcomes.

04Sampling Strategy

Field Study Sampling

The field study employs purposive selection of review-active hotel markets, followed by cross-platform matching and eligibility screening.

The five study destinations were chosen to provide hotels with sufficient review activity, variation across leisure markets, and reliable matching opportunities between TripAdvisor and Expedia.

The primary sample excludes hotels that do not meet the minimum recent-review threshold, consistent with TripAdvisor's review-volume-related eligibility criteria for AI summaries.

Main sample: 188 hotels.

Broader robustness sample: 638 matched hotels with at least one text review during the study window.

Experimental Sampling

The experimental component uses online participants recruited through Prolific.

Participants were allocated to controlled review environments, and the final experimental samples were analysed using the same core outcome measures across conditions.

Sample Quality Control

Participants were required to complete the study and pass a predetermined attention check.

The main experiments used an instructional attention-check item asking participants to select response option 6.

The reported attention-check exclusions were:

  • Study 2a: 4 participants.
  • Study 2b: 2 participants.
  • Study 3: 3 participants.

Further quality and validity checks were conducted using manipulation checks and supplementary analyses.

05Questionnaire Design

The online experimental questionnaires were designed to measure review-writing intentions and anticipated recognition after exposure to an assigned review environment.

The structure followed a common sequence:

  1. Consent
  2. Scenario
  3. Review Environment
  4. Outcome Measures
  5. Recognition Measures
  6. Checks & Respondent Information

Questionnaire Preview — Actual Measurement Items

Section C1. Willingness to Leave a Review

Question

"Please indicate your willingness to write a review about the restaurant on this website."

Response scale: 1 = Not interested at all; 7 = Extremely interested.

The product and coffee shop experiments adapted the scenario wording accordingly.

Section C2. Review-Writing Effort

Question stem

"If you were to leave a review on this website..."

Participants evaluated three items:

  1. How much effort would you put into writing the review?
  2. How much thought would you give to the review when writing it?
  3. How much time would you spend writing the review?

Response scale: 1 = Not at all; 7 = Very much.

The three items were combined to form a composite measure of intended writing effort.

Section D1. Anticipated Review Helpfulness

Question

"If you were to leave a review on this website, to what extent would you expect other people to find your review:"

  • Useful
  • Informative
  • Helpful

Response scale: 1 = Not at all; 7 = Very much.

This measure captures whether potential contributors anticipate their reviews being valuable to other consumers.

Section D2. Anticipated Social Recognition

Question stem

"If you were to leave a review on this website, you would expect that:"

  • Many people would read your review.
  • Other people would appreciate what you write.
  • Other people would read what you write carefully.
  • Your contribution would matter a lot to other people.

Response scale: 1 = Strongly disagree; 7 = Strongly agree.

This broader operationalization was included in Studies 2a and 2b. Study 3 retained anticipated review helpfulness as its measure of recognition rather than collecting this alternative social-recognition measure.

Section E. Manipulation and Attention Checks

The experimental questionnaires included checks to determine whether participants understood the assigned review environment.

For Studies 2a and 2b, participants were asked whether the review webpage contained an AI-generated summary.

An attention-check item instructed participants to select response option 6.

In addition, a separate pretest of Study 3's experimental manipulations assessed whether participants perceived the intended differences in AI-summary presence and highlighted reader engagement.

Questionnaire Design Principles

Random Assignment

Experimental conditions were manipulated to support comparisons between review environments.

Consistent Measurement

The principal outcome measures were administered across conditions to support comparable analyses.

Controlled Information

Study 2a and its replication controlled the informational content of the manipulated review presentation.

Multiple-Item Measurement

Writing effort and anticipated recognition were measured using multi-item constructs, improving measurement reliability relative to single-item alternatives.

Validation

Manipulation checks, attention checks, and additional validity analyses provided evidence relevant to interpreting the experimental findings.

06Research Quality and Design Limitations

Manipulation Validation

The studies included procedures to evaluate whether participants noticed the experimental treatments.

Studies 2a and 2b used direct checks of AI-summary presence.

A separate pretest with 101 participants examined the two manipulated factors in Study 3: AI-summary availability and highlighting reader engagement.

Design Limitations

Observational Identification

Study 1 relies on the assumptions of the difference-in-differences design, including parallel trends and the absence of relevant spillovers. Cross-platform comparisons may not eliminate all platform-specific changes.

Experimental Generalizability

The experiments use hypothetical review-writing scenarios and self-reported behavioural intentions and effort rather than observing all participants making real-world review contributions.

Mechanism Interpretation

Mediation analyses assess statistical indirect effects consistent with the proposed role of anticipated recognition. They do not independently establish definitive causal mediation.

Sampling Boundaries

The field study concentrates on selected U.S. hotel markets, while the experiments recruit online participants. Findings should therefore be generalized to other populations, products, and platforms with appropriate caution.

07Research Design Summary

The research design integrates a real-world platform comparison with three preregistered online experiments.

The field study measures actual review contributions following the introduction of AI-generated summaries. The experiments evaluate review-writing intentions, the psychological role of anticipated recognition, and the potential moderating effect of highlighting reader engagement.

Together, these complementary methods establish a structured basis for examining both the behavioural and psychological consequences of AI-powered review summarisation.
