AI-generated review summaries · Step 2 of 5

Developing the Approach

How a platform management challenge becomes a theory-driven, testable research strategy.

01Overview

Building on the problem identified in Step 1, this project adopts a multi-study quantitative approach to examine how AI-generated review summaries (AIGS) influence consumer review-writing behaviour.

The research integrates real-world platform data with controlled experiments. The field study examines changes in actual review contributions following the introduction of AI summaries, while three preregistered experiments investigate the underlying psychological mechanism and a potential platform design intervention.

Together, these approaches connect observed consumer behaviour with experimentally tested psychological explanations, producing insights relevant to online review platform management.

Research at a Glance

The three preregistered experiments below are the core experimental studies; supplemental experiments are addressed under robustness and boundary conditions.

02Theoretical Framework

The research approach combines three complementary perspectives to explain why a feature that benefits review readers might negatively affect review writers.

1. Information Processing and Reader Behaviour

Why might consumers rely on AI summaries rather than individual reviews?

Consumers frequently encounter large volumes of information when evaluating products and services. AI-generated summaries reduce the effort required to process this information by synthesizing multiple consumer perspectives into a concise overview.

Drawing on research on information processing and decision-making shortcuts, the project considers how AI summaries might shift reader attention away from individual contributions.

This change in anticipated reader behaviour provides the starting point for examining the consequences for review writers.

2. Social Recognition Theory

Why does being noticed and appreciated matter to review writers?

Social Recognition Theory suggests that individuals value acknowledgement and appreciation from others.

Online review writing is not simply an information-sharing activity. By contributing their opinions and experiences, consumers may also seek confirmation that their input is useful, meaningful, and valued.

When AI summaries become prominent, potential contributors may anticipate that fewer readers will engage with individual reviews. As a result, they may expect less recognition for their contributions.

This project therefore proposes anticipated recognition as a psychological mechanism linking the presence of AI summaries to reduced review-writing motivation.

3. Reader Engagement as a Platform Design Intervention

Can platforms maintain a sense of recognition even when AI summaries are present?

If the negative effects of AI summaries arise partly because contributors expect less recognition, platforms may be able to mitigate those effects by highlighting opportunities for reader engagement.

Visible indicators such as helpful votes, comments, and acknowledgements signal that individual reviews continue to receive attention and appreciation.

The research examines whether highlighting these forms of engagement reduces the negative association between AI summaries and anticipated recognition, thereby supporting continued review contribution.

03Research Objectives and Measurement Logic

Building on the theoretical framework, the research is structured around six objectives. Each objective connects a platform management question to measurable outcomes and an appropriate analytical strategy.

FrameworkResearch ObjectiveMeasurementAnalysisBusiness Insight

01. Real-World Review Contribution

Business question: Does the introduction of AI-generated summaries change actual consumer review contributions?

Key measures

  • Daily number of text reviews
  • Total words contributed per day
  • AI-summary introduction period
  • Platform and hotel identifiers

Analysis direction

Use a cross-platform difference-in-differences design comparing review activity for matched hotels on TripAdvisor and Expedia before and after TripAdvisor introduced AI-generated summaries.

Working expectation

The availability of AI-generated summaries is expected to reduce review contributions.

Business relevance

Evaluate whether introducing AI-powered review features may have unintended consequences for the supply of consumer-generated content.

02. Willingness to Leave Reviews

Business question: Does the presence of AI summaries discourage consumers from writing reviews?

Key measures

  • AI-generated summary presence versus absence
  • Willingness to leave a review
  • Service and product contexts

Analysis direction

Conduct controlled experiments comparing consumers' willingness to leave a review across AIGS and control conditions. Use ANOVA to test differences between experimental groups.

Working expectation

Consumers exposed to AI-generated summaries are expected to report lower willingness to leave reviews.

Business relevance

Understand the potential influence of AI-powered review presentation on future content participation.

03. Review-Writing Effort

Business question: Does the presence of AI summaries affect how much effort consumers are willing to put into their reviews?

Key measures

  • Intended writing effort
  • Thought invested in writing
  • Time intended for writing

Analysis direction

Compare writing-effort scores between experimental conditions using group mean comparisons. Examine whether the pattern is consistent across service and product settings.

Working expectation

The presence of AI-generated summaries is expected to reduce intended review-writing effort.

Business relevance

Assess potential implications for the amount and richness of user-generated content, beyond the number of reviews submitted.

04. Anticipated Recognition

Business question: Why might AI summaries discourage consumers from contributing reviews?

Key measures

  • Anticipated review helpfulness
  • Anticipated social recognition
  • Willingness to leave reviews
  • Review-writing effort

Analysis direction

Evaluate differences in anticipated recognition across experimental conditions and conduct mediation analyses to assess whether reduced anticipated recognition statistically accounts for changes in review-writing behaviour.

Working expectation

AI-generated summaries are expected to reduce anticipated recognition, which in turn is associated with lower willingness to contribute and reduced writing effort.

Business relevance

Identify a psychological process that platforms could address when developing AI-powered review features.

05. Reader Engagement and Platform Design

Business question: Can highlighting reader engagement help maintain review contributions when AI summaries are present?

Key measures

  • AI summaries: present versus absent
  • Highlighting reader engagement: present versus absent
  • Anticipated recognition
  • Review-writing willingness and effort

Analysis direction

Use a 2 × 2 between-subjects experiment and test the interaction between AI summaries and highlighting reader engagement. Examine the proposed conditional indirect effect using moderated mediation analysis.

Working expectation

Highlighting reader engagement is expected to weaken the negative indirect association between AI-generated summaries and review-writing behaviour through anticipated recognition.

Business relevance

Evaluate whether making individual feedback and acknowledgements more visible could mitigate the negative effects of AI summaries.

06. Robustness and Boundary Conditions

Business question: Are the effects limited to particular product categories, experimental settings, or platform features?

Key considerations

  • Service versus product reviews
  • Alternative presentations of review summaries
  • Summary authorship
  • Positive versus negative consumption experiences
  • Review rating valence
  • External incentives

Analysis direction

Use supplemental experiments and robustness analyses to examine the consistency of the main effect and identify circumstances under which its magnitude may change.

Research objective

Assess the robustness, generalizability, and potential boundaries of the proposed relationship between AI-generated summaries and review-writing behaviour.

Business relevance

Help identify where the evidence may be most relevant for different platform settings and content-management strategies.

Analytical Standards

MethodPurpose
Difference-in-differencesEstimate changes in actual review contributions following the introduction of AIGS
ANOVACompare experimental condition means
Mediation analysisTest indirect associations through anticipated recognition
Moderated mediationExamine the role of highlighted reader engagement
Robustness checksAssess the stability of findings across specifications and settings

Testing standard: 95% confidence intervals, with 5,000 bootstrap samples used in the reported mediation analyses. Field estimates use standard errors clustered at the hotel level.

Interpretation Guardrails

The field analysis relies on assumptions required for difference-in-differences identification, including parallel trends and the absence of relevant spillovers.

The experimental studies provide evidence about how consumers respond to manipulated review environments. Indirect effects are interpreted as evidence consistent with the proposed psychological mechanism, rather than definitive proof of causal mediation.

The research does not estimate direct sales effects, platform profitability, or the financial return on investment from implementing AI summaries.

04Project Plan

The research was implemented through a sequence of complementary evidence-generation stages, progressing from real-world platform analysis to controlled experimental testing and robustness assessment.

Research Implementation Roadmap

Stage 1 — Field Evidence and Data Preparation

Collect and organize review histories from TripAdvisor and Expedia, match hotel listings across platforms, define the observation window, and construct the hotel-by-platform-by-day dataset.

Stage 2 — Experimental Development

Develop experimental stimuli representing review environments with and without AI-generated summaries. Translate the proposed psychological mechanism into measurable survey constructs.

Stage 3 — Experimental Data Collection

Conduct online experiments across service and product settings, followed by a factorial experiment examining the role of highlighted reader engagement.

Stage 4 — Statistical Analysis and Validation

Clean and prepare the collected data, evaluate condition differences, estimate indirect and conditional indirect effects, and conduct robustness analyses.

Stage 5 — Interpretation and Reporting

Synthesize findings across field and experimental evidence and translate them into research conclusions and practical considerations for online review platforms.

Research Deliverables

The project produces several complementary outputs.

1. Field Evidence Report

A cross-platform assessment of changes in review volume and text production following the introduction of AI-generated summaries.

2. Experimental Findings

Comparisons of willingness to review, writing effort, and anticipated recognition across manipulated review environments.

3. Psychological Mechanism Assessment

Evidence examining anticipated recognition as a proposed mediator of consumers' review-writing responses.

4. Platform Design Evaluation

An assessment of whether highlighting reader engagement can reduce the negative effect associated with AI summaries.

5. Robustness and Generalizability Assessment

Additional experimental and analytical evidence assessing the consistency and boundaries of the findings.

6. Research and Managerial Implications

A synthesis of findings with recommendations for balancing the benefits of AI-assisted information processing with the continued generation of consumer reviews.

Project Resource Note

Unlike a simulated commercial research portfolio, this project is based on research that has already been conducted using real-world platform data and experimental participants.

Accordingly, the project plan documents the implemented research workflow rather than proposing an invented recruitment budget or commercial project timeline.

05Approach Summary

This research approach connects a real platform management challenge to a series of measurable consumer-behaviour questions.

By combining observational field evidence with controlled experiments, the project investigates not only whether AI-generated review summaries influence review-writing behaviour, but also why these effects may emerge and how platform design could potentially mitigate them.
